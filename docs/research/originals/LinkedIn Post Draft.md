# LinkedIn Post Draft

We’re likely looking at the largest volume of whiskey aging in barrels in modern history. But mapping exactly where it all sits reveals some fascinating data gaps. 

For a project on DistilleryMap.org, I set out to build a world map of current maturing inventory. I wanted to move past production capacity and look strictly at what’s resting in the warehouses right now.

Here is what the latest credible disclosures show:
🥃 **Scotland:** ~22 million casks (SWA, 2026)
🥃 **USA (Kentucky):** A record 17.1 million barrels of spirits, primarily Bourbon (KDA, 2026). Nationally, American whiskey inventory sits at nearly 1.5 billion proof gallons (DISCUS).
🥃 **Ireland:** ~4.5 million casks—nearly nine years of global supply (LYQD Irish Whiskey Supply Report 2026).
🥃 **England:** Forecasting ~50,000 casks by the end of 2024 (EWG).
🥃 **Tasmania:** Lark alone reported 2.4 million litres maturing at the end of 2025. 

The challenge? Comparing physical casks to standardised barrels and tax-based proof gallons is notoriously difficult. And for major producers like Canada and Japan, while we know companies like Suntory and Hiram Walker hold hundreds of thousands of casks, finding a defensible national aggregate is surprisingly tough. 

This map isn’t a definitive census—it’s a conversation starter. 

At DistilleryMap.org, we’re mapping over 6,100 distilleries globally through community contribution. If you have better data for the “data gap” regions or want to refine these estimates, I’d love to hear from you. 

Where do you think the biggest hidden inventories are sitting today?
